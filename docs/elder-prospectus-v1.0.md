## Something is Coming

We do not need to argue about whether South Africa faces a deepening crisis. Anyone paying honest attention can see it. The question is not whether communities will be tested — it is whether they will be ready.

When formal systems fail — when the grid goes down for weeks not hours, when municipal water stops, when supply chains fracture, when people cannot access their bank accounts — the communities most at risk are those already living at the margins. The ones the formal economy has never fully included. The ones who have been surviving on resilience they built themselves, largely without support, for generations.

These communities do not lack intelligence. They do not lack resourcefulness. They do not lack the will to help one another.

What they lack is infrastructure. A way to see what they collectively have. A way to connect what one person carries with what another person needs. A way to hold their knowledge, their relationships, and their gifts in a form that doesn't disappear when a crisis hits.

ResilientSA is being built to provide that infrastructure.

## What ResilientSA Is

ResilientSA is a community resilience platform rooted in Ubuntu philosophy — designed to activate the natural gifts of South African communities, build dense networks of mutual support through existing community structures, and sustain life and dignity when formal systems fail.

It is built on a simple and ancient insight:

> *Every community already holds what it needs. The work is to make that visible, connect it, and trust it.*

The platform organises community life around six fundamental pillars of resilience: **Water, Food, Health, Safety, Energy, and Skills & Trade.** Within these pillars, community members exchange goods, skills, knowledge, and services — not through money, but through a gift economy grounded in Ubuntu values. What you have, you offer. What you need, you ask for. The community holds the exchange in trust.

It is built on the structures communities already have — Resident Associations, Community Police Forums, neighbourhood networks — rather than creating new ones. It works offline as well as online, through SMS as well as smartphones, through printed directories as well as digital maps. It is designed for the people who need it most, not the people with the best connectivity.

This is not a technology project that has borrowed Ubuntu language. It is an Ubuntu project that uses technology carefully, humbly, and only where it genuinely serves.

## The Role of Wisdom in This

There is something ResilientSA cannot build with code.

Communities in crisis do not only need resources. They need knowledge — the kind that lives in people, not in systems. They need the agronomist who knows which crops survive drought. The nurse who knows how to stretch limited medicines. The legal mind who knows what rights communities hold even when the state withdraws. The psychologist who understands trauma and can help a community stay coherent under sustained pressure. The educator who can keep children learning when schools close. The community pioneer who has spent twenty years building trust in places where trust is scarce.

This knowledge exists. It lives in remarkable people across South Africa — academics, researchers, practitioners, activists, change makers — many of whom have spent careers trying to reach communities with exactly what those communities need, often against significant institutional friction.

ResilientSA is being designed, in part, to remove that friction.

## What the Platform Offers People Doing This Work

The platform includes a network of what we call **Grounders** — people and organisations with funded programmes, specialist knowledge, and a genuine mandate to serve communities — who can connect directly with community nodes on the platform.

A community in the Northern Cape struggling with water scarcity can find and invite a hydrogeologist running a borehole programme. A township community in Gauteng can connect with an urban agriculture specialist. A rural community in KwaZulu-Natal can access a cooperative development practitioner who helps them build the legal and financial structures to interface with the formal economy on their own terms.

The platform handles the infrastructure of that connection:

- A verified profile that presents your work honestly and accessibly to communities
- A matching system that surfaces your programme to communities whose needs align with what you offer
- An impact reporting layer that auto-generates the evidence your funders need — as a natural by-product of your engagement, not as an additional administrative burden
- A peer network of other Grounders whose work complements yours
- A community feedback mechanism that holds you accountable to the people you serve — not to a platform algorithm

Communities choose who enters. They evaluate impact. They decide whether a programme serves them. The platform gives Grounders reach. Communities give Grounders purpose.

## What This Is Not

It is worth being direct about what ResilientSA is not, and what it is not yet.

It is **not built yet.** This is a vision being shared to find the people who should help shape it before the building begins. The architecture is designed. The philosophy is clear. The legal and cooperative frameworks are mapped. The build has not started.

It is **not a technology solution to a human problem.** The technology is the least important part. The most important part is the network of human relationships — between community members, between communities, between communities and the people carrying knowledge they need. The platform exists to support those relationships, not to replace them.

It is **not extractive.** No community data is sold. No Grounder's impact data is commercialised. No individual's gifts profile is profiled for advertising. The platform is governed by a council of community representatives. It is open source. It belongs to no one and therefore to everyone.

It is **not another well-meaning project that arrives in communities uninvited.** Communities choose to join. Communities choose which Grounders enter. Communities set their own terms of exchange. The platform facilitates what communities decide. It never decides for communities.

## Why Now

The honest answer is that the right time to build resilience infrastructure is before the crisis, not during it.

South Africa's communities — particularly those already under economic pressure — do not have the luxury of waiting to see how bad things get. The July 2021 unrest showed, in concentrated and devastating form, what happens when communities are left without coordination infrastructure in a moment of collapse. What is coming may be slower, broader, and longer-lasting.

The platform needs to be in communities, functioning, trusted, and woven into daily life before it is needed in a crisis. That takes time. That is why the building must begin now — with the right people, the right foundations, and the right relationships already in place.

## Who I Am and Why I Am Building This

My name is Deon (Sketch) Hanns. I am a visual storyteller, consciousness explorer, and founder of The San Scribe.

My first book — *San Consciousness: The Return of the First Wisdom Keepers* — explores the ancient intelligence of the First People and what it means to reconnect with that wisdom in the modern world. At the heart of that work is a conviction I have carried for years: that the knowledge systems which sustained human communities for millennia — systems built on reciprocity, on gift, on deep relationship with land and with each other — are not relics. They are exactly what is needed now.

ResilientSA grows directly from that conviction. If the First Wisdom Keepers understood that community, reciprocity, and the gifting of knowledge are the foundations of human resilience — then my work is to build the infrastructure that allows that understanding to function at the scale our communities now need.

I am a designer and a builder. I am not a development economist or a community organiser or an academic. What I bring is the ability to hold complexity, to make the invisible visible, and to build systems that serve human beings rather than replacing them.

## A Final Word

The San people — Africa's First People — survived for tens of thousands of years in conditions that would have broken communities built on different foundations. They survived because they understood something we are only now beginning to remember:

*That the gift freely given is the most powerful economic act. That the knowledge shared loses nothing and gains everything. That a community which knows itself — its gifts, its needs, its relationships, its history — is a community that cannot be broken.*

ResilientSA is an attempt to build, in modern form, what the First People always knew.

If that matters to you — if your work is already an expression of that understanding — then perhaps we are already, in some sense, building this together.

I look forward to the conversation.
